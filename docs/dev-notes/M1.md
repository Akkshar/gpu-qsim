## Order of work

### 1. `insert_zero_bit` → `[bits]`
The whole simulator rests on this. Draw it: split k into "bits below pos" and "bits from pos up",
shift the upper part left by one, OR them back together. A mask of the low `pos` bits is `(1 << pos) - 1`
(as a 64-bit value, careful with `1 << 40` on a 32-bit int).

### 2. Gate matrices → `[gates]`
Straight from the formulas in `gates.hpp`. `std::polar` / `std::exp` with a complex argument help.
The tests compare **exactly**, global phase included, because a controlled gate exposes the phase.

### 3. `apply`, 1 target, no controls → `[apply]`
For target t there are 2^(n-1) pairs. For pair k: `i0 = insert_zero_bit(k, t)`, `i1 = i0 | (1 << t)`,
then `(a0, a1) ← M · (a0, a1)`. Read both before writing either.

Things to get right:
- `op.matrix` is `complex<double>`; `amps_` may be `complex<float>`. Convert the four entries once
  per op, not per amplitude.
- Why not `for i in 0..2^n: if (i & (1<<t)) continue;`? It works, but half the iterations do nothing
  and every one branches. You'll need the branch-free version on the GPU, so start with it here.

### 4. Controls → `[controls]`
Two ways; do the first, then think about the second (you'll want it in M2):
- **Mask check:** build `cmask` = OR of `1 << c` over controls; skip a pair unless `(i0 & cmask) == cmask`.
  Simple, but with 2 controls you skip 3/4 of the pairs.
- **Insert more bits:** iterate only over pairs whose control bits are 1, by inserting a 0 at every
  target *and* control position (in ascending order!) and then OR-ing in `cmask`. No wasted iterations.

### 5. Several targets → `[multi]`
For k targets: 2^(n-k) groups of 2^k amplitudes. For each group:
1. `base` = the group index with a 0 inserted at every target position (insert in **ascending** qubit
   order, or the positions shift under you), then OR in the control mask.
2. The 2^k member indices: member m sets target bit `targets[j]` iff bit j of m is set. Note this uses
   `targets` in **matrix order** (`targets[0]` = low bit of m), not sorted order. The `mat` tests with
   `3 1` and `4 0 2` targets are there to catch exactly this mix-up.
3. Gather the 2^k amplitudes, multiply by the 2^k × 2^k matrix, scatter back.

Precompute the 2^k offsets once per op; they don't depend on the group.

Once general k works, the 1-qubit path from step 3 is a special case. Keep it anyway as the fast
path (measure the difference with `qsim_cli`; that's your first BENCH.md entry of M1).

### 6. Measurement → `[measure]`
- `norm_squared`, `probabilities`: accumulate in `double` even for float states.
- `sample`: cumulative sum of probabilities, then for each shot draw u ∈ [0, total) and binary-search
  (`std::upper_bound`). Seed a `std::mt19937_64` with `seed`. Using `total` rather than 1.0 keeps it
  right when rounding leaves the sum at 0.9999999.
- `measure(q)`: p1 = Σ|amp|² over indices with bit q set. Draw, then zero the half that didn't
  happen and scale the rest by 1/√p.

### 7. Cross-check
```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe tests\qiskit_check.py check --circuits 200
.venv\Scripts\python.exe tests\qiskit_check.py check --circuits 200 --precision f32
.venv\Scripts\python.exe tests\qiskit_check.py check --circuits 500 --seed 2 --max-depth 100
```
Failures are saved to `tests\failures\`; shrink one by deleting lines until it still fails.

### 8. CPU baseline numbers → BENCH.md
```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe tests\qiskit_check.py emit --qubits 24 --depth 200 --seed 7 --out bench\circuits\rand24.qc
.\build\Release\qsim_cli.exe bench\circuits\rand24.qc --reps 5
```
Record 16/20/24 qubits, f32 and f64: ms per op and the "full-pass equivalent GB/s". Compare that GB/s
with your RAM's bandwidth. How far off is a single thread? (OpenMP is M4; this is the single-thread
reference point.)
